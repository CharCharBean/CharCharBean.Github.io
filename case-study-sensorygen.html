Case Study

SensoryGen

Turning a rare, EPA-registered natural insect repellent into a defensible go-to-market and premium exit strategy.

Role
Market & Commercialization Strategy
Timeline
Spring 2026
Team
2-person team
Context
Blackstone LaunchPad Challenge
Executive Summary

SensoryGen was about to secure one of only a handful of EPA-registered natural repellent actives in existence — but had no revenue, brand, or distribution, and no clear way to monetize it. I analyzed three commercialization pathways — sell, license, or launch — against market sizing, regulatory scarcity, royalty sensitivity, and comparable acquisitions. The resulting hybrid strategy reframed the asset from a ~$15–40M outright sale toward a projected $30–65M+ exit, and placed 2nd in the Blackstone LaunchPad Challenge.

The Context

The challenge

SensoryGen, a UCR-spinout biotech, was on the verge of earning U.S. EPA registration for Mithral — a natural insect repellent active claiming nearly double DEET's protection time. That registration is an extraordinarily rare event: only one new natural active had been registered in roughly 25 years, and there is no abbreviated regulatory pathway, making it a genuine competitive moat.

The problem was that scarcity on its own does not guarantee value. SensoryGen was pre-revenue, with no brand or shelf presence, entering a market tightly controlled by incumbents like SC Johnson and Spectrum Brands. The central question was whether the company should sell the IP outright, license it for upfront fees plus royalties, or invest in an independent launch to prove demand first. Each path carried a very different risk profile, capital requirement, and ceiling on value — and the window to negotiate from strength was narrow.

The Framework

My approach

01

Size the market & the moat

Mapped the global and U.S. natural repellent market, its growth versus the DEET segment, and quantified why an EPA-registered active is so difficult to replicate.

02

Model the pathways

Built risk/reward profiles for sell vs. license vs. launch, ran a royalty sensitivity analysis, and benchmarked against comparable deals — AgraQuest, Burt's Bees, and Evolva's nootkatone.

03

Design the deal

Recommended a phased hybrid strategy, mapped 12+ potential buyers across four tiers, and designed the competitive-tension tactics needed to drive premium terms.

The Findings

Insights & recommendations

What the analysis showed

  • EPA-registered natural actives are exceptionally rare — roughly one in 25 years — so Mithral is a regulatory moat, not just a product.
  • Scarcity alone doesn't command a premium: Evolva's nootkatone sold at a discount without traction, while a regulated active paired with proven demand (AgraQuest, ~10× revenue) commands top multiples.
  • Recommendation: run a lean DTC/Amazon launch to generate commercial proof, in parallel with segmented (territory + field-of-use) licensing talks — manufacturing competitive tension to push royalties to 6–8% with escalators.
The Outcome

Business impact

The project reinforced how much of a scientific asset's value lives outside the lab. Mithral's efficacy was real, but the difference between a salvage-value sale and a premium exit came down to leverage and timing — proof of demand, competitive tension among buyers, and monetizing while momentum is strongest. Translating a regulatory milestone into a sequenced, evidence-backed deal strategy is where the value was created, and it's the part of the work that earned a 2nd-place finish.
